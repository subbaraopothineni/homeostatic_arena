# Pre-submission inquiry to the editors (draft; you send it)

**Why send it.** A short, open inquiry before submission tells the editors about the AI use
and your independent status up front, and asks directly whether the paper suits the
journal. Editors often reply within days. A "yes" lowers the risk of desk rejection, and a
"no" saves you the wait.

**Where to send it.** Use the editorial-office contact given on the *Artificial Life*
pages (https://direct.mit.edu/artl, "Contact" or "Editorial Board"). No address is filled
in below, on purpose: take it from the journal's own site.

**Before sending:**
- Replace every `[FILL]`.
- Read the whole text and put it in your own words where you want to.
- Send it from the address you will use for the submission.

---

**Subject:** Pre-submission inquiry: minimal mechanisms and behavioural markers of sentience (Article)

Dear Editors,

I would be grateful for your view on whether the manuscript described below is suitable for *Artificial Life* before I submit it.

**Title:** Behavioural Markers of Sentience Are Met by Minimal Mechanisms: Validated and Pre-Registered Tests With Homeostatic Agents

**Summary.** Behavioural markers developed for animal sentience are increasingly proposed as evidence about artificial systems. Using a physics-simulated homeostatic agent, I show that each behavioural marker I tested is met by a mechanism with at most one bit of memory, and that two standard measurements are misleading. I also report a pre-registered, prediction-based test of the sense of agency, validated with positive, negative and shortcut controls and a sensitivity analysis. Learning agents did not acquire the capacity it measures. The work follows the artificial-life approach of testing claims with minimal synthetic agents (about 7,400 words; code, data and pre-registrations public).
